Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. For Jersey City homes, that means we stop the source, pull the water out, seal off wet areas and get the structure stable. When this stage goes right, there is less to rebuild afterward, occasionally nothing at all.

People often use mitigation and restoration to mean the same thing. Each is its own phase of the job. The emergency stage is mitigation, where we stop the loss, keep it from spreading and stabilize the house. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

Four tasks make up most mitigation visits. One: control the source and cut off any new water. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. We also protect furniture and belongings by moving or blocking them up.

Water Damage Mitigation Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Downspouts should empty well clear of the foundation, and gutters need to stay free of debris.
  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Seal gaps around windows, doors and roof penetrations before the next heavy rain arrives.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • In cold months, a heated house protects pipes from freezing, even while you travel.
  • Schedule an annual water heater inspection and swap the unit out before its tank gives way.

The Benefits of Professional Water Damage Mitigation

Mitigation is a series of judgment calls made quickly. Which drywall can be dried in place and which has to be cut out? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? We answer those questions with training and moisture readings rather than by the look or feel of a surface.

There is a claim-related reason to do mitigation right as well. Most policies require the owner to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, and insurers want proof it was done. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. Together those records show the water's effect and our response to it. It also gives the repair contractor a clear starting point, with no wet surprises behind new drywall.

Our Water Damage Mitigation Process

Our systematic approach delivers quality results on every project. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

1

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. If a plumber or roofer is needed for the permanent fix, we coordinate the timing.

2

Extract the Water

We extract standing water using pumps and extraction units, then check carpet and its padding. Fast removal of bulk water limits damage more than drying equipment could by itself.

3

Contain Wet Areas

Wet rooms are closed off from dry ones with barriers of plastic sheeting and controlled airflow. This keeps humidity, dust and any contaminants from travelling through the house while work is under way.

4

Stabilize the Structure

Unsalvageable materials are removed, surfaces get antimicrobial treatment, and air movers and dehumidifiers are set. Moisture readings are recorded on every visit until the structure can be repaired, typically in 3 to 5 days.

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Since the leak you have noticed puffed-up baseboards or gaps opening behind them.
  • The insurance company instructed you to take reasonable action against further damage.
  • A leak has been stopped but floors, walls or cabinets are still wet.
  • Water went beneath kitchen or bath cabinets, out of reach of towels and fans.
  • A musty odor has begun because materials stayed wet for a day.
  • Carpet squishes underfoot, or the pad beneath it is saturated.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
